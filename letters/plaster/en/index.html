A Sticking Plaster

On New Year's Day, 2027, I received a line from my seven-year-old son.

廣東話繁體中文English
Published 2026.10.21   /   5 min read   /   Story date 1 January 2027
One line. Two moments in time.

On New Year's Day, 2027, I was clearing out a drawer.

Once a year. Every time, I promise myself I won't keep so much useless stuff. Then I spend two hours moving it all to another drawer.

That was how I found the diary again. A grey-blue cover, curled corners, a white crease along the spine. 2017.

I'd bought it for my son to practise his Chinese. We'd agreed on the terms: he didn't have to write every day. One line was enough. If he couldn't write, he could draw.

I opened the first page and found a line of writing.

Dad, is it raining where you are?

The Chinese character for "Dad" was too large. The four dots inside "rain" were squeezed together. The question mark at the end looked like a worm trying to stand upright.

I stared at that line for a long time.

My son was in the next room. I called to him.

"Just a minute."

His voice was deep now.

The hand that had written the line in front of me must have been too small to hold a pen steadily.

· · ·

I picked up a pen and wrote underneath:

What year is it where you are?

When I'd finished, the paper grew a little warm.

At first I thought my hand had warmed it. I moved my hand away and waited. The page stayed warm.

A pencil line slowly appeared. First a rectangle, then two little circles. Underneath came rows of crooked numbers.

A calendar. 2017. A very large circle around the first of January.

I immediately began another line: "Over here, it's—"

There was ink in the pen. But not a single word would stay on the page.

I tried three pens. Same result. On the back of a receipt, every one of them worked.

I turned to the flyleaf and found the promise I'd written ten years earlier.

One line is enough.

"You're going to hold me to that too?"

From the next room: "Who are you talking to?"

"No one."

· · ·

I adjusted my grip and drew another calendar. 2027.

The other side went quiet for a long time.

Then a person slowly appeared. Glasses, a few strands of hair, an enormous belly. An arrow beside him pointed at me.

I laughed out loud.

"Even in ten years, I don't need to look quite that bad."

I drew two lines across the belly, trying to slim it down. The other side filled it straight back in and added a belly button.

Next came a suitcase. Its lid was open. It was completely empty. He drew a question mark beside it.

I recognised that way of drawing.

When I travelled for work, he used to pack for me. Afterwards he'd sit beside the case, checking one item at a time for anything missing. He worried more than I did that I'd get to Japan without any tissues.

I'd meant to draw him something from the future. A very thin computer. Or what he would look like when he grew up.

But he'd already started packing.

First a packet of tissues. Then an oddly shaped bottle of loquat cough syrup. A sticking plaster. Two books.

One book was too thick for the case to close. He rubbed out part of the edge and made the suitcase bigger.

I watched him work.

Outside, it began to rain.

My son called again from the next room. "What did you want me for?"

"Just a minute."

I reached to turn the page, to see whether there was any space further on.

Something fell onto the table.

A sticking plaster.

A real one. A corner of its wrapper was folded over, with a few pencil shavings stuck to it.

I didn't move.

On the other side of the diary, someone tapped twice, softly, with the tip of a pencil.

· · ·

I put the plaster at the corner of the table, opened my computer and found the blog I'd written back then.

Search: luggage.

December 2016. Four days in Japan. The post was short, only a few lines.

I looked at the screen, then back at the diary.

That wasn't right.

The time he'd packed for me had been before New Year's Day. But the calendar on the other side had the first of January circled.

Perhaps he'd drawn it wrong. Perhaps the child just wanted to play at packing again.

I found the photographs from that year. December: the airport, Japan, a hotel breakfast. January the first: at home, on the sofa, with him in his pyjamas.

I hadn't travelled for work that day.

I pulled the diary closer. Beside the suitcase, the other side had added another picture: an aeroplane and three people.

One wore glasses. One had long hair. One was very small, with a hand raised above his head.

I had no memory of the journey he was drawing.

I was about to take the diary into the next room when half of the child on the page was suddenly rubbed away.

When he was drawn back in, his arm was lowered, close to his side.

A little later, the aeroplane was erased too.

My hand stopped on the door handle.

· · ·

"Dad?"

My son was already in the doorway. He looked at the table, then at me.

"Did you hurt yourself?"

I followed his gaze. While moving the cardboard boxes, I'd somehow cut the side of my index finger. Only now did it begin to sting.

He picked up the plaster from the corner of the table, opened it and handed it to me.

"What did you need me for?"

I took it. It was a moment before I answered.

"Have a look at this drawing for me."

He pulled up a chair. I turned the diary towards him.

His first reaction was to laugh.

"Who drew you? It's a pretty good likeness."

Then he looked at the half-empty suitcase, and his smile slowly faded.

He pointed to a tiny square on the page. I'd always thought it was the lock on the case.

"That's a window."

I looked down again.

Two wheels. An open lid. A question mark beside it.

If you turned the notebook through ninety degrees—

It was a door.

Inside it, someone had just turned on the light.
